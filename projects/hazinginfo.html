Project Overview

HazingInfo.org is a national transparency platform that structures data on college hazing incidents. My role focused on designing the data architecture and transparency indicators used to standardize institutional reporting.

My Role

I worked as a Student Assistant on a multidisciplinary team composed of legal researchers, policy experts, and technical collaborators. My primary responsibility was leading database architecture decisions and supporting UX design that shaped how complex institutional data could be presented clearly to the public.

I acted as a bridge between research workflows and technical implementation, ensuring that data collection practices aligned with how information would ultimately be surfaced to users.

Individual Contributions

I designed and maintained the platform’s database structure, defining how institutional records, transparency indicators, and incident documentation were standardized across thousands of entries.

A major contribution was developing internal documentation outlining how to locate and verify hazing-related information across university websites.

These standards enabled scalable data collection, improved consistency across contributors, and strengthened long-term sustainability of the project.

Problem & Opportunity

Hazing transparency data is highly fragmented. Universities publish policies, reporting forms, and incident disclosures in inconsistent locations and formats, making cross-institution comparison difficult and inconsistent.

Process & Solution

The project followed an iterative research and design process combining data modeling, UX design, and continuous validation.

Outcome & Next Steps

The platform now serves as a centralized national resource that enables users to evaluate institutional hazing transparency practices.

Standardized data structures improved consistency and reduced onboarding time for new contributors.
