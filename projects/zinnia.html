Zinnia All Day

Dementia Care Content Scheduling Prototype

Zinnia Technologies · Capybara Capstone Team · Jan–Spring 2026

Project Overview

Zinnia All Day is a capstone project focused on designing a prototype that helps dementia caregivers schedule video content across a day in ways that support routine, reduce distress, and improve overall engagement.

The project was conducted in collaboration with Zinnia Technologies and scoped to deliver meaningful design and evaluation value within the constraints of an academic Winter–Spring quarter timeline.

Problem & Opportunity

In memory care settings, television is frequently used as a passive tool, often without intentional scheduling or alignment with patient routines. Caregivers lack simple, flexible tools to organize content across a day in ways that account for cognitive load, time-of-day needs, and emotional regulation.

This creates an opportunity for a scheduling interface that supports caregivers while allowing organizations like Zinnia to evaluate new content engagement strategies.

My Role & Team Context

I worked as part of a multidisciplinary capstone team partnering with Zinnia Technologies. While the sponsor provided high-level goals and ongoing feedback, our team was responsible for defining scope, conducting independent research, and designing the prototype.

My primary contributions focused on research synthesis, problem framing, system constraints definition, and translating insights into concrete user flows and prototype requirements.

Research Approach

Independent Capstone Research

Due to data privacy constraints around sponsor-led research, our team conducted independent research to meet capstone requirements. This included interviewing caregivers and individuals with experience in memory care, at-home healthcare, and related environments.

Research was intentionally framed around general caregiving scenarios rather than Zinnia-specific workflows, allowing insights to be transferable and ethically collected.

Sponsor Research as Supplement

Where possible, high-level findings from sponsor-affiliated research (e.g., Duke University focus groups) were used as supplementary context rather than primary evidence.

Scope & System Constraints

To remain feasible and secure, the project was scoped as a prototype without live integration into Zinnia’s production systems.

  • Simulated backend to demonstrate scheduling logic
  • No access to internal databases or user data
  • Focus on UX workflows, not deployment

This allowed the prototype to function as a proof of concept suitable for user validation and internal evaluation.

Design Process

The design process was guided by recurring questions around value, scalability, ethics, and accessibility:

  • How do caregivers naturally think about time-of-day routines?
  • How can scheduling reduce, rather than increase, caregiver burden?
  • What ethical considerations arise when designing content for cognitively vulnerable users?

Insights from research and sponsor feedback were translated into user flows, low- to mid-fidelity wireframes, and a proposed scheduling interface that emphasized clarity, flexibility, and predictability.

Outcome & Next Steps

The Winter quarter concluded with a design-focused prototype demonstrating the core scheduling concept and visual analytics Zinnia was interested in exploring.

Planned Spring quarter work includes refining the prototype, conducting user validation, and exploring how a lightweight functional implementation could support further evaluation without requiring live system integration.

Key Takeaways

  • Designing for dementia care requires balancing structure with flexibility
  • Clear scoping is essential when working with real-world sponsors
  • Ethical considerations must be embedded early in system design
